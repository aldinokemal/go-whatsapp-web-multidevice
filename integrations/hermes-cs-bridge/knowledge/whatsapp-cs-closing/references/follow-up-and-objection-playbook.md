# Follow-up and objection playbook

## Intent-based 30-day cadence

This is a ceiling, not a quota. A reply returns the lead to the appropriate live-conversation stage; it never triggers the next scheduled touch automatically.

| Point | Send only when | Purpose | Message shape | Stop/branch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | A question or recommendation is awaiting a small decision | Let the lead choose the next useful detail | “Would you like me to check `[JADWAL]` or summarize `[HARGA]` first?” | Wait after one message |
| Day 1 | No response to a meaningful answer | Confirm receipt, invite one low-effort question | Mention the exact earlier topic, offer 2–3 helpful choices | A reply resumes discovery |
| Day 3 | CRM names a specific obstacle | Resolve one named barrier | Pricing, timing, family-summary, fit, or process branch | Never send generic promo |
| Day 7 | A real verified update exists | Add actual value | New matching schedule, confirmed answer, or genuine availability | Skip entirely without new value |
| Day 14 | Lead may be losing interest but has not refused | Reset permission | Offer a clear “boleh kabari” option and state that silence ends outreach | Silence ends sequence |
| Day 30 | Explicit opt-in remains valid and a relevant update exists | Light reactivation | Refer to the earlier need and one current relevant option | `tidak dulu` becomes do-not-follow-up |

## Hard stops

Immediately stop automated follow-up and set the CRM to do-not-follow-up when the lead says: `tidak`, `belum dulu`, `stop`, asks not to be contacted, complains about messages, or indicates the offer is unsuitable. A single polite closure is allowed if it acknowledges the request; further sales content is not.

Do not send Day 7 merely to maintain cadence. Do not send Day 30 to a silent Day-14 lead unless they gave explicit permission to receive later updates.

## LURC response patterns

### Price

**Clarify:** “Yang terasa berat itu nominalnya, cara bayarnya, atau Kakak masih ingin memastikan kecocokan kelasnya?”

**Respond:** Restate only current verified price/frequency options. Match the lightest viable option to the lead’s realistic time and budget; do not invent a discount, installment, or concession.

**Confirm:** “Apakah ini sudah menjawab pertimbangannya, atau ada bagian lain yang perlu saya pastikan?”

### Schedule or busyness

**Clarify:** “Waktu yang paling realistis biasanya pagi, siang, malam, atau hari tertentu?”

**Respond:** Check actual availability. If nothing fits, say so and offer a legitimate waitlist/alert only if it exists.

### Consistency concern

**Clarify:** “Dari ritme Kakak sekarang, komitmen yang terasa mungkin itu seperti apa?”

**Respond:** Recommend the most realistic frequency, not the maximum package. Do not promise attendance or results.

### Already studying elsewhere

**Clarify:** “Bagian apa yang ingin Kakak tambahkan atau kuatkan dari proses belajar yang sudah berjalan?”

**Respond:** Position the offer only if it fills a genuine gap; otherwise decline to push it.

### Online-learning doubt

**Clarify:** “Pengalaman atau kekhawatiran apa yang membuat Kakak ragu belajar online?”

**Respond:** Describe the verified learning process and its limits. Do not claim online delivery is universally equal to offline delivery.

### Needs family approval

**Respond:** Offer a concise forwarding summary: objective, process, schedule, price, and registration step. Give time and ask whether a single check-in is welcome.

### Trust, teacher, credential, policy, or class size

**Respond:** Do not generalize. Check the exact documented fact for the exact class and give a deadline for the verified reply.

### Not ready / polite deferral

**Respond:** “Baik, saya tutup follow-up dulu agar tidak mengganggu. Jika suatu saat ingin melanjutkan, cukup balas chat ini.”

Set the hard-stop CRM state. Do not try to turn a deferral into a negotiation.

## Transcript audit checklist

1. Did the CS answer the direct question promptly?
2. Did it discover enough to recommend, without asking redundant questions?
3. Did the recommendation explicitly connect to the lead’s stated need?
4. Did the CS distinguish facts from pending checks?
5. Did it ask one clear next step?
6. Was follow-up connected to the previous barrier rather than a new generic pitch?
7. Was any “not now” or opt-out honored immediately?
8. Did an internal system error leak to the lead?
9. Is the CRM next action and permission state complete?
